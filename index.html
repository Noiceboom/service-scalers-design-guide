02 · Principles

Confident, specific, never generic.

Service Scalers sells to home services owners — plumbers, HVAC techs, electricians, roofers. They see a hundred marketing agencies a year and every one of them talks the same. Our brand's job is to sound like the account manager they wish they had, not like a pitch deck.

01 / 04

Numbers do the selling.

Real dollars, real dates, real cost-per-lead. Never a percentage without a base. Never "significant improvements" when we can say "+34% in 60 days."

Proof over promise
02 / 04

Hand-made, on purpose.

The hard offset shadow, the 25px card radius, the Voltage script — all of it says a person made this. Not a template. Not a growth-hacker.

Warm over slick
03 / 04

One accent per view.

One orange CTA. One Voltage phrase. One hero KPI. When everything shouts, nothing lands. Restraint is the differentiator.

Signal over noise
04 / 04

Plain English, always.

Contractors don't want "omnichannel synergy." They want more calls and fewer no-shows. Write the way we'd say it on a call.

Clear over clever

The single most important rule

Every number that matters is set in Open Sans Condensed 800 with line-height: 1 and letter-spacing: 0.3px. KPI values, counts, dollars, percentages, phone numbers, dates. This is the strongest brand signal in marketing work. Never set a headline or a KPI in plain Open Sans; never set body copy in Condensed.

06 · Voice & Copy

Talk like the account manager they wish they had.

A good AM at Service Scalers is plain, specific, and never hedges. They own bad news, name the fix, and use the client's own numbers. That's the voice — for landing pages, decks, ads, email, and everything in between.

Tone
CorporateNeighborly
Tempo
WordyShort
Polish
FolksyPro-clean
Persuasion
HypeNumbers
Do
Cost per lead dropped 18% this month.

Concrete number, in the client's language, no adjectives needed. This is what a good AM says on a monthly call.

Don't
Experience significant lead-gen improvements.

Every home-services agency writes this sentence. It signals nothing. Cut the abstraction and use a real number.

Do
Stop counting leads, start counting checks.

Confronts the actual pain — leads that don't turn into revenue. Uses Voltage script correctly: one emphasized phrase.

Don't
Unlock scalable, omnichannel synergy today.

Four buzzwords in seven words. If a home-services owner read this out loud, they'd close the tab.

Do
Google response expected by May 18. We'll text you the second it's back.

Owns a problem, names the fix, gives a date. Client fear (uncertainty) is met with a specific next step.

Don't
Your service will be restored soon.

Vague on purpose — which reads as hiding. "Soon" is the worst word in a client email. Give the date or don't send.

Label conventions
How labels read

Uppercase micro-labels are terse noun phrases: "COST PER LEAD", "AVG MAP RANK", "UPTIME (30D)". Sub-lines are always comparisons: "+12 vs last month", "of $9,000 budget", "↓ $39 vs last month". Never mix a verb into a micro-label.

Status labels are exactly: Running well · Needs attention · At risk · Setting up. Don't invent variants ("Optimizing," "In-flight," "Pending review") — they break the shared vocabulary between marketing and app.

Headline recipes
Three patterns that always work

Contrast pair: "More calls, not more busywork." · "Real leads, not just clicks." · "Stop counting leads, start counting checks."

Timeframe + number: "34 more calls in 60 days." · "$8,420 in ad spend, 47 booked jobs."

Owned problem + fix: "Google flagged your LSA. We appealed the same day."

The Signature Shadow

The hard offset shadow is the single most identifying element of the marketing surface. Zero blur. Solid Charcoal. Presses down on hover. Nothing else in the system uses shadow — no soft drops, no glow, no elevation ramps.

Small

Motion
Ease and durations

Easing: cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.46, 0.45, 0.94). Durations: 150ms fast (color, opacity), 300ms base (cards, hover shift), 500ms slow (section reveals). Cards press down on hover — 2px translateY, shadow collapses to 0 1px 0 0 #202526. Nothing else animates in the client app; on the marketing surface, only hover feedback and the live-visitor pulse.

11 · Photography & Illustration

Real trucks, real people. No stock.

Photography is where most home-services marketing goes wrong. Stock photos of smiling actors in stiff blue polos read as generic. We shoot (or source) real work: trucks in driveways, techs mid-diagnosis, owners at their desk, a well-lit portrait of the crew. Always warm, always documentary — never posed against a white studio backdrop.

[ CREW · DRIVEWAY · GOLDEN HOUR ]
Hero Portrait

Owner + crew, warm afternoon light. Truck in frame. Documentary — no forced smiles.

[ MID-JOB · TIGHT CROP ]
Craft in Progress

Hands, tools, work in-frame. Show the actual job — not "smiling at a wrench."

[ OWNER · OFFICE · CANDID ]
Client Portrait

Owner in their own environment. Coffee, papers, phone ringing. Warm not corporate.

Photo direction
Six rules for every shoot & every stock pull

1. Warm light — golden hour or tungsten, never fluorescent. 2. Real people from the actual business. 3. Environment matters — truck, driveway, kitchen, workshop. 4. Tight crops on hands and tools; wide crops on portraits with breathing room. 5. No forced eye contact with the camera; documentary beats posed. 6. Color-grade toward warm — pull cyan out of shadows, push amber into highlights.

Never
Photos that break the brand on sight

Stock actors in matching corporate polos. Handshake photos. "Diverse business team pointing at a laptop." Anything shot on a pure white backdrop. Any photo with a lens flare added in post. Anything from a stock site that has "successful business team" in the metadata.

12 · Guide vs. Reality

Where the current site drifts from this guide.

A guide only matters if the surfaces catch up. Below is the current gap analysis between what this guide specifies and what servicescalers.com actually ships today. None of these are wrong — they're just decisions the guide changes going forward.

Critical
The site is currently dark-first; the guide is cream-first

The live servicescalers.com uses a dark charcoal background with cream text as its default page treatment. This guide (and the v1.0 Brand & Product guide it inherits from) specifies the marketing surface as warm cream page (#fffbf2) with charcoal text — dark treatments are reserved for the proof band, footer CTA, and one hero art block per page.

What to change: Rebuild the default page background to Body Cream. Keep dark sections as intentional accents (below-hero proof band, above-footer CTA, one dark card in the case-study section). This is the single biggest visual shift and the change that will make future pages feel like a family, not one-off designs.

Flag
Hard offset shadow isn't present on the live site

The site currently uses either no shadow or a soft blur drop shadow. The hard offset shadow (0 5px 0 0 #202526) is the strongest single identifier of the Service Scalers marketing brand — it's what makes cards feel hand-made instead of templated. Apply it to every card, button, testimonial, and offer block.

Flag
Voltage script isn't yet in play

The site headlines currently sit in a single sans-serif face without the script accent. The Voltage highlight is what turns a headline from "confident" into "on-brand." Add one Voltage phrase to hero headlines and every section title where a natural emphasis exists ("not more busywork", "ring different", etc.).

Add
Missing sections the guide expects

Proof band: The current site has social proof but not in the four-tile band format defined here. That block is the fastest read on any Service Scalers page. Case study block: Named clients with real numbers. Owner quote card: Cream card, gold stars, name + business + city. All three exist in the guide but are absent or under-formed on the live site.

Add
Collateral is undefined outside the website

The current brand guide (v1.0) covers the marketing card system, but doesn't specify slide templates, one-pager fliers, ad creative frameworks, or email design. This guide adds all four. Save the slide templates as a Keynote / PowerPoint file and the fliers as InDesign / Figma sources under Desktop/Service Scalers Brand/Collateral/.

Note
What this guide does NOT redefine

Everything the v1.0 Brand & Product guide already covers stays authoritative: exact hex codes, App-surface rules, the KPI typography contract, motion tokens, and the eight-color status system. This guide extends that work into marketing collateral. If the two guides ever conflict on tokens, the v1.0 guide wins.
